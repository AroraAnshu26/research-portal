## THE ONE THING

A NeurIPS 2026 paper shows a capability audit can be defeated with a proof.
Draguns, Motwani, Douglas and Schroeder de Witt embed encrypted neural networks
inside host model weights, decrypting and executing them "only upon receiving a
secret trigger". The hidden circuits "remain provably hard to elicit and
interpret under cryptographic assumptions, even given full access to model
weights". Fine-tuning, weight noising, gradient-based trigger search
and latent adversarial training all failed to elicit them on a chemical reaction
task, "a proxy for CBRN-relevant capabilities". The stated result: "a limit of
elicitation-based capability audits".
([arXiv 2610.13928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13928)) · *thesis · paper, NeurIPS 2026 · single-source*

`bears_on` assurance-seam, which needs a party able to state with validity what
a learned system does. It bounds what weight access buys.

## MOVES

**A monitor's accuracy was separated from its worth.** CSIRO's RESCUE restores simulator and policy states at execution checkpoints and compares alternative actions across 128,340 counterfactual branches, on OpenVLA-OFT and π0.5 over LIBERO-Spatial and LIBERO-Plus. "Favourable detection scores do not establish lower intervention cost." Under a stop-and-reset model, "even an idealised detector can afford only a 2.0-3.3% false-alarm rate before unnecessary resets outweigh the savings from stopping failures early". ([arXiv 2610.13516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13516)) · *thesis · paper · single-source*

**A replay bench reached physical hardware.** JitterVLA "replays identical commands and arrival traces through alternative controllers", across hand trajectories from 20 people, three open VLA pipelines at 838 episodes, and a physical UR3. Interpolation lowered modeled p99 jerk 83%, from 615 to 106 m/s^3, at 200 ms delay and a 2.0 cm tracking cost. On the UR3 it lowered accelerometer-derived jerk 3.0 to 14.0%. What is replayed is the command stream, not contact under a changed action. ([arXiv 2610.13628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13628)) · *thesis · paper · single-source*

**A paper wrote down what a governance answer must contain to be checkable.** Rosenbacke et al. hold that "a checkable answer needs a named person, a date, a number, and a stop that has been used or tested", and define control as someone who "can detect a serious problem, challenge the decision, stop the system and reverse it, and who has something to lose by not doing so". ([arXiv 2610.13340](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13340)) · *thesis · paper, no number · single-source*
