Three observations

1. The Ré pipeline is a machine, and it runs on PhDs. Cartesia, Engram, Numbers Station, Rox and Radical Numerics all have a Chris Ré lab member as a founder, and Ré himself as co-founder on four of them. Cartesia's four founders were all his PhD students and founded the day they graduated. No master's student appears anywhere in that pipeline.

2. The undergrad route is voice, video and analytics — not research. Willow, Human Behavior, Golpo, Known, Phia, Verita, TensorPool. Cheque sizes $0.5M–$10M, almost all YC or a named angel network. The exception that proves the shape: Wispr Flow, founded straight out of Stanford undergrad in 2021, is now reported at $2B — it took five years and a full pivot.

3. Dorm-floor and hacker-house proximity keeps showing up as the founding mechanism. TensorPool's three founders met on the same freshman dorm floor. Human Behavior's team met at a hacker house one founder started after leaving. Phia was two roommates. Vori's three met at Stanford. This is the part that is not replicable from off campus, and it is the reason to be physically present in those rooms.

Where these companies actually came from

Ordered roughly by how low the barrier is. The first one has no selection at all — admission is guaranteed to any Stanford student who fills in the form — and it exists specifically to help you find co-founders.

Stanford Venture Studio

Any Stanford-affiliated student

Admission is non-competitive — guaranteed to any Stanford student who submits the application form. 24/7 coworking, compute credits, mentoring, and mixers explicitly for founding-team formation. Recently expanded with dedicated AI labs giving student teams compute.

StartX

Stanford students, faculty and alumni

Zero equity, no fees. Reported $120B total portfolio valuation, average $42M raised per company; ~123 startups supported as of May 2026 with 5 unicorns and 32 exits; wider community 1,300 companies and 20 unicorns.

Cardinal Ventures

Stanford student teams

10-week program run twice a year (fall and spring), 12-20 teams admitted, weekly speaker sessions and structured workshops.

NFX FAST for Stanford

Stanford founders

Either a $150K discovery check on an uncapped SAFE, or a $1.5M-$3M pre-seed/seed for 15%. Application deadline 12 April. NFX partners Pete Flint, Anna Piñol and Valerie Osband Mahoney are Stanford alumni. NFX was first investor in Mammoth Biosciences, Arc, Jupiter, SESO — all Stanford.

Pear Garage / Pear Dorm

Engineering students at Stanford and Berkeley

Pear VC runs Garage for Bay Area engineering students and Dorm for student builders; winning teams get a $100K uncapped SAFE and a fast-track PearX partner meeting. Pear was the seed investor in Valar Labs (PearX S21).

Neo Scholars and Neo Residency

Students showing early technical promise

Neo Scholars: ~30 students a year, $20K grant plus mentor network — every one of OpenAI's new-grad hires in a recent year was a Neo scholar. Neo Residency: 5-8 students get $40K no-strings to take a semester off. Neo backed Cursor and Applied Compute.

Y Combinator Early Decision

Students who want to finish their degree

Launched Sept 2025: apply now, get funded the moment you are accepted, then do YC after graduation. In W26 Stanford had 22 founders in the batch (Berkeley 30, Harvard 18).

Z Fellows

Technical builders of any age, including students

One-week intensive, optional $10K investment at a $1B valuation cap. Many Stanford and MIT students participate while still enrolled.

a16z Speedrun

Any founder, heavily young-founder weighted

Up to $1M plus $10M+ in credits, 12 weeks in San Francisco. Acceptance rate below 0.4%. Over $180M deployed to 150+ startups.

Thiel Fellowship

Under-23s willing to leave school

$250,000 over two years in the 2026 class (up from $200K in 2025 and $100K before). 2026 cohort: 12 founders from 7 countries, including a Stanford CS student who was an MTS at OpenAI on agent memory and a Stanford AI Lab researcher. Prior fellows founded Anthropic, Cognition, Etched, Figma, Mercor.

Breakthrough Ventures

College students and recent grads

Student-run. Up to $100K grants, Microsoft and NVIDIA compute, $50K follow-on, Demo Day at Stanford.

AI for Healthcare Bootcamp (Stanford ML Group)

Stanford students

Its alumni started Valar Labs (a16z, $26M) and Wispr AI ($2B). This is the single highest-yield student program found in this pass relative to its size.

TreeHacks

Anyone, Stanford-hosted

12th annual edition in Feb 2026 awarded $500,000 in prizes, up from $200,000 in 2025. Sponsored by OpenAI, Google and Anthropic.

Stanford Horizons

Robotics and drone builders

Stanford robotics drone hackathon — the hardware-side counterpart to TreeHacks.

DAF-Stanford AI Studio

Students interested in defense AI

Department of the Air Force and Stanford joint studio. Context: Stanford's first defense-tech career fair drew 300 students, Stanford DEFCON drew 1,000+ attendees, and Stanford Women in National Security has 150+ members.

BASES

Undergrads, entry via Frosh Battalion in early fall

Runs the $100K pitch competition; you can apply to help run programs at the end of the school year.

ASES

Stanford students, founded 1989

12-week design-thinking bootcamp for freshmen culminating in a pitch to Silicon Valley investors.

Stanford Biotechnology Group / Students in Biodesign / SPARK

Bio-side students

The bio equivalents of the CS entry points; SPARK runs the translational-medicine startup pipeline.

How this was built, and what is missing

Inclusion rule

A company is in if at least one founder was a Stanford undergrad, master's student, PhD candidate or postdoc at founding, or left that programme to found it. Founders with more than roughly five years of prior industry are excluded per the brief — which is why World Labs (Fei-Fei Li, faculty), Physical Intelligence (faculty plus DeepMind veterans), SambaNova, Together AI and Snorkel are not here despite being Stanford companies. They belong to the faculty-and-operator layer, not the student layer.

Verified out

Four companies that look Stanford-student on first pass and are not, checked and discarded: Noble Machines (Apple, Caltech and NASA veterans — the founder is Wenlong Ma, not Stanford's Wenlong Huang), Density AI (ex-Tesla Dojo team), Deep Cogito (IIT Delhi, ex-Google Search), Goodfire (Yale, NYU, DeepMind). Mercor, often filed under Stanford, is Georgetown and Harvard dropouts.

Known gaps

This is a floor, not a census. There is no registry of student-founded companies. Stanford does not track why students take a leave of absence, so nobody — including Stanford — knows the real number. One dropout founder put his own count of classmates who left for AI companies at about a dozen; a Stanford economics professor separately said he knew of at least five in his own classes.

Undisclosed rounds. Candor, Revere Technologies, SoranoAI and 10x Science have no public funding figures. Known and Golpo disclose amounts but not investors. Axiom Math's investor names were not in any source I found.

Under-covered by construction. Press coverage skews to consumer AI and to founders who make good copy. Hardware, bio and defence student companies are almost certainly undercounted here: SPARK and the Biotechnology Group run real pipelines whose outputs rarely get a TechCrunch post. Treat the domain mix in the Patterns tab as a map of media attention as much as of activity.

One unresolved name. The 2026 Thiel class includes a Stanford CS student, ex-OpenAI on agent memory and Stanford AI Lab researcher, now COO of Kinetic — reported only by surname. I could not confirm the full name or the company.
