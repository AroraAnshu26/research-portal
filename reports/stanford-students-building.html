Three observations

Stanford is about 8% of the YC directory. 252 of 3,014 companies from Winter 2022 onward have a founder whose own bio names Stanford. That is a base rate, not a shortlist, and it is the number to hold in mind when anyone describes a Stanford YC founder as rare.

The master's route is not unusual here. Master's and undergraduate ties are the two largest determinable categories after the generic ones. Whatever the faculty-spinout pipeline looks like, the YC layer is dominated by people who were students and left, not by people who finished a doctorate first.

B2B is roughly half of it. Healthcare and Industrials follow. The consumer-AI skew that press coverage produced is an artefact of what gets written about, not of what gets built.

Where these companies came from

Ordered roughly by how low the barrier is. The first has no selection at all — admission is guaranteed to any Stanford student who fills in the form — and it exists specifically to help you find co-founders.

Stanford Venture Studio

Any Stanford-affiliated student

Admission is non-competitive — guaranteed to any Stanford student who submits the application form. 24/7 coworking, compute credits, mentoring, and mixers explicitly for founding-team formation. Recently expanded with dedicated AI labs giving student teams compute.

StartX

Stanford students, faculty and alumni

Zero equity, no fees. Reported $120B total portfolio valuation, average $42M raised per company; ~123 startups supported as of May 2026 with 5 unicorns and 32 exits; wider community 1,300 companies and 20 unicorns.

Cardinal Ventures

Stanford student teams

10-week program run twice a year (fall and spring), 12-20 teams admitted, weekly speaker sessions and structured workshops.

NFX FAST for Stanford

Stanford founders

Either a $150K discovery check on an uncapped SAFE, or a $1.5M-$3M pre-seed/seed for 15%. Application deadline 12 April. NFX partners Pete Flint, Anna Piñol and Valerie Osband Mahoney are Stanford alumni. NFX was first investor in Mammoth Biosciences, Arc, Jupiter, SESO — all Stanford.

Pear Garage / Pear Dorm

Engineering students at Stanford and Berkeley

Pear VC runs Garage for Bay Area engineering students and Dorm for student builders; winning teams get a $100K uncapped SAFE and a fast-track PearX partner meeting. Pear was the seed investor in Valar Labs (PearX S21).

Neo Scholars and Neo Residency

Students showing early technical promise

Neo Scholars: ~30 students a year, $20K grant plus mentor network — every one of OpenAI's new-grad hires in a recent year was a Neo scholar. Neo Residency: 5-8 students get $40K no-strings to take a semester off. Neo backed Cursor and Applied Compute.

Y Combinator Early Decision

Students who want to finish their degree

Launched Sept 2025: apply now, get funded the moment you are accepted, then do YC after graduation. In W26 Stanford had 22 founders in the batch (Berkeley 30, Harvard 18).

Z Fellows

Technical builders of any age, including students

One-week intensive, optional $10K investment at a $1B valuation cap. Many Stanford and MIT students participate while still enrolled.

a16z Speedrun

Any founder, heavily young-founder weighted

Up to $1M plus $10M+ in credits, 12 weeks in San Francisco. Acceptance rate below 0.4%. Over $180M deployed to 150+ startups.

Thiel Fellowship

Under-23s willing to leave school

$250,000 over two years in the 2026 class (up from $200K in 2025 and $100K before). 2026 cohort: 12 founders from 7 countries, including a Stanford CS student who was an MTS at OpenAI on agent memory and a Stanford AI Lab researcher. Prior fellows founded Anthropic, Cognition, Etched, Figma, Mercor.

Breakthrough Ventures

College students and recent grads

Student-run. Up to $100K grants, Microsoft and NVIDIA compute, $50K follow-on, Demo Day at Stanford.

AI for Healthcare Bootcamp (Stanford ML Group)

Stanford students

Its alumni started Valar Labs (a16z, $26M) and Wispr AI ($2B). This is the single highest-yield student program found in this pass relative to its size.

TreeHacks

Anyone, Stanford-hosted

12th annual edition in Feb 2026 awarded $500,000 in prizes, up from $200,000 in 2025. Sponsored by OpenAI, Google and Anthropic.

Stanford Horizons

Robotics and drone builders

Stanford robotics drone hackathon — the hardware-side counterpart to TreeHacks.

DAF-Stanford AI Studio

Students interested in defense AI

Department of the Air Force and Stanford joint studio. Context: Stanford's first defense-tech career fair drew 300 students, Stanford DEFCON drew 1,000+ attendees, and Stanford Women in National Security has 150+ members.

BASES

Undergrads, entry via Frosh Battalion in early fall

Runs the $100K pitch competition; you can apply to help run programs at the end of the school year.

ASES

Stanford students, founded 1989

12-week design-thinking bootcamp for freshmen culminating in a pitch to Silicon Valley investors.

Stanford Biotechnology Group / Students in Biodesign / SPARK

Bio-side students

The bio equivalents of the CS entry points; SPARK runs the translational-medicine startup pipeline.

Why the first version of this board said 28

Because it was built from press coverage. The first pass searched news for Stanford student founders and found 28 companies. Press covers the loud ones: large rounds, famous surnames, consumer products. Enact (Summer 2026, real-world RL environments for robotics, CTO out of Chelsea Finn's IRIS lab) and Innate (Fall 2024, personal AI robots, CEO a former Stanford HCI researcher) were both absent, and both are obvious once you look at the directory rather than at the news. An order-of-magnitude undercount is the expected result of that method, not bad luck.

What replaced it

The systematic layer. YC's Algolia index carries no founder data at all, which is why a census had to go further. Each company page, however, embeds a JSON blob with founders[], including a self-written founder_bio. All 3,014 pages were fetched, yielding 5,830 founder records of which 5,690 (97.6%) carry a bio. A company qualifies when a founder's bio names Stanford as a personal affiliation.

Not a grep. Two error classes showed up in a sanity pass and both are handled. First, wrong tie kind: "Deep RL at Stanford and PhD at Technical University Munich" was being labelled PhD, so tie kinds are now read only within ±150 characters of the word Stanford, and a nearest-university rule discards degrees that belong to another school — that alone corrected Bloomy (undergrad was Amherst) and PAX Markets (undergrad was the Naval Academy). Second, wrong company: "working with top scientists from Stanford, Adobe and Brown ... during his time at IIT Kharagpur" is a collaboration, not an affiliation, so collaboration phrasing is subtracted before the personal test. One company was excluded on that basis.

Precision, measured rather than asserted. On a random sample of 14 counted companies, all 14 were true positives at the company level once the full bio was read — including four whose evidence was only visible beyond the first line, such as the founder with "4 degrees from Stanford in just over 6 years". Tie-kind accuracy is lower than company accuracy, which is why the tables above report a large "unspecified" bucket instead of forcing a category.

What is still missing, and it is not small. The YC snapshot starts at Winter 2022, so earlier Stanford YC companies are invisible to it — Vori is a confirmed example, caught only because the curated layer had it. 13 companies are held in an ambiguous bucket in the data file awaiting a human read. 23 YC companies publish no founder records at all. Bios are self-written, so a Stanford founder who did not mention it is uncountable. And the non-YC side is still press-derived: the 22 curated-only companies are real, but there is no reason to believe they are all of them. This board is a floor in both layers, and the YC layer is the only one with a defensible denominator.

Reproducing it

tools/yc/scrape.py → founders.py → stanford_filter.py → build_students.py. The founder fetch caches every page under .cache/yc-founders, so a re-run costs only what has changed. stanford_yc.json carries the evidence window and the affiliation verdict for every founder record, so any row here can be argued with by pointing at the text it came from.
