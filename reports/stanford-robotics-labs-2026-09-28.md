# Stanford robotics: every lab, every PI, and the actual way in

Retrieved and verified 2026-09-28. Scope: every Stanford research group with people doing
robotics, across Computer Science, Electrical Engineering, Aeronautics & Astronautics,
Mechanical Engineering, Bioengineering, Civil & Environmental Engineering, Chemical
Engineering, Management Science & Engineering, Oceans and the School of Medicine.

The point of this document is the **intake column**. Every form URL below was read off the
lab's own page today, and every audience label ("Stanford UG/MS", "postdoc", "visiting") was
read from the sentence the link sits in — not inferred from the form title. Where no form
exists, the row says so rather than guessing one.

### Three patterns worth knowing before you write anything

1. **The three robot-learning labs run the identical funnel.** IRIS, ILIAD and IPRL each
   publish exactly three forms — postdoc, Stanford UG/MS, visiting — with near-identical
   wording, and all three explicitly ask prospective *PhD* applicants **not** to email before
   admission. The form is the only channel that exists. A cold email to Finn, Sadigh or Bohg
   about PhD admission is a channel they have said in writing they will not answer.
2. **Course-first labs.** ASL (Pavone) admits through a **quarter-long research rotation**,
   and its FAQ names the prerequisite sequence: AA 274A *Principles of Robotic Autonomy I*
   (autumn), then AA 203 *Introduction to Optimal Control Theory* (spring), do well, then
   rotate — summer is named as the best rotation window. SIA Lab (Bansal) likewise says
   Stanford students are "strongly encouraged" to take AA 276 *Principles of Safety-Critical
   Autonomy* before starting research there. For these two, the course is the application.
3. **The arrive-then-ask labs.** Biomechatronics (Collins) states the sequence plainly:
   apply to the ME MS programme, enrol, arrive in autumn, *then* email him. Several ME labs
   work this way and publish no form at all.

## 14. Method, and what this list is not

**Three sources, deliberately overlapping.**

1. **The SRC roster and its 18 lab pages**, fetched today. This is the only place Stanford
   itself enumerates robotics across school boundaries, and it is where the Medicine, Oceans
   and Psychiatry seats come from.
2. **Department lab and faculty registers**: `me.stanford.edu/research-impact/labs`,
   `aa.stanford.edu/research-impact/labs-and-centers`, `me.stanford.edu/people/faculty`,
   `aa.stanford.edu/people/faculty`.
3. **The Stanford Profiles census engine**, still live:
   `profiles.stanford.edu/browse/school-of-engineering?affiliations=capFaculty&ps=100&p=N`
   returns server-rendered faculty with the free-text research-interest field, parseable from
   `<li class="mini-profile-holder">` blocks. 695 School of Engineering faculty harvested
   across 7 pages, filtered on a robotics keyword set (robot, manipulat, autonomy, haptic,
   exoskelet, prosthe, drone, unmanned, teleoperat, embodied, locomotion, actuator, humanoid,
   self-driving, motion planning, multi-agent, swarm, mechatronic, UAV). 59 hits.

**The keyword sweep is a floor, not a list, and the reason is measurable.** Only about 59% of
Stanford faculty fill in the research-interest field at all. Dorsa Sadigh, Mac Schwager, Iro
Armeni, Gordon Wetzstein, Zerina Kapetanovic, Emma Brunskill, Nick Haber, Chris Gerdes and
Thomas Berrueta are all unambiguous robotics people and **none of them came back from the
keyword sweep** — they are here because a lab page or department register carries them. Any
number derived from that census is a lower bound.

**What is deliberately excluded.** Keyword false positives were read individually and dropped:
"optical manipulation" (Solgaard, Brongersma), "manipulation of molecular transport" (Garten),
"manipulating atomic structure" (Reidy). Emeriti are listed but marked, because an emeritus
roster entry is not an intake. Adjacent rows are labelled "(adjacent)" rather than silently
promoted — a controls or MEMS or materials lab is a real route into robotics, but it is not a
robotics lab and the column should not pretend otherwise.

**Known gaps in the intake column.** Several lab sites are client-side-rendered and returned no
usable links to an HTTP client — The Movement Lab, NAV Lab, SLAB, DDL, Gradient Spaces, the
Computational Imaging Lab, Autonomous Agents, Guibas Lab. For those the row says "email"
because no form was *found*, which is not the same as no form *existing*. CHARM's People page
says "Interested in joining the CHARM Lab? Please see this page" and the target link did not
resolve from the raw HTML, so CHARM may have a join page this document is missing. Two probes
worth repeating in a browser pane rather than over HTTP: `shape.stanford.edu` answers 404 to a
plain HTTP client but is live and indexed, and `kapetanovic.sites.stanford.edu` returned 503
while `s4lab.stanford.edu` resolved cleanly.

**One roster fact that will age fastest.** Thomas Berrueta starts 1 November 2026 with no
students. PAL Lab's prospective-member form is live now. That window closes as the lab fills.
