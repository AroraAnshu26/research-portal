Robotics & physical AI

What is observably happening

Stanford opened a dedicated Robotics Center in autumn 2024 with six test bays built as a kitchen, bedroom, hospital and warehouse. Its faculty roster of 42 includes people appointed in Oceans, Pathology, Surgery, Radiology, Chemical Engineering and Civil Engineering, not only CS, EE, ME and Aero/Astro. The centre is an overlay on existing departments rather than a new department.

Who is paying

Venture capital for the spinouts (CapitalG, Thrive, Lux, Kleiner Perkins, Lightspeed, Sumitomo); federal grants for the labs; corporate affiliate fees via SystemX, whose 35 members include Apple, TSMC, Samsung, Bosch, Caterpillar and Tencent.

Market size, as published

Humanoid robots in 2026 estimated at $5.41B (MarketsandMarkets), $6.09B (Straits Research) and $6.24B (Fortune Business Insights), with 2026-2034/35 CAGRs quoted between 28.1% and 50.6%. A separate industry report puts the total robotics market at $38B in 2026.

The arithmetic

Physical Intelligence ($5.6B confirmed) plus Dexterity ($1.65B) equals $7.25B of Stanford-linked private valuation. Against the $38B stated total robotics market that is 19.1%; against the $5.41B-$6.24B humanoid market it is 116%-134%. My calculation. Both companies together are therefore valued at more than the entire current humanoid market they are usually grouped with, which means the valuations are priced on future capability rather than present revenue.

World models & spatial AI

What is observably happening

World Labs went from a $1B seed valuation in September 2024 to $5B in February 2026, and shipped Marble publicly on 12 November 2025. Fei-Fei Li's stated thesis is that language models have no native model of geometry, persistence, occlusion or gravity, so the LLM paradigm is the wrong shape for robots and simulated environments.

Who is paying

NVIDIA, AMD and Autodesk are all strategic rather than purely financial investors; Autodesk put in $200M and took a strategic advisory role. That is chip vendors and a CAD incumbent buying position in the same category.

Market size, as published

No established third-party market-size series exists for 'spatial intelligence' or 'world models' as a category. The adjacent measurable pools are CAD/simulation software and game engines, neither of which the research firms have relabelled.

The arithmetic

$5B of valuation against zero published category market size. My observation, not an estimate: this cluster is priced entirely on the thesis, because there is no revenue series to divide by.

AI compute, chips & infrastructure

What is observably happening

Two of the largest Stanford-faculty-founded companies are both in the compute layer rather than the model layer: SambaNova (Olukotun, Re) and Together AI (Re, Liang). H.-S. Philip Wong has been Chief Scientist of TSMC in an advisory capacity since 2020 while remaining Stanford faculty.

Who is paying

Hyperscaler capital expenditure, and General Atlantic and Salesforce Ventures at the equity level. The five largest operators (Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta, Oracle) have guided to a combined $725B-$800B of 2026 capex, roughly three times the ~$238B deployed in 2024, of which about 75% is AI-specific.

Market size, as published

2026 hyperscaler capex $725B-$800B guided. Of combined hyperscaler AI capex, roughly 55-60% is reported to flow directly or indirectly to NVIDIA, 7-9% to AMD, and 8-10% to TSMC for custom silicon.

The arithmetic

SambaNova ($11B) plus Together AI ($8.3B) equals $19.3B of Stanford-linked private valuation, which is 2.4%-2.7% of a single year of guided hyperscaler capex. My calculation. Put the other way: the alternative-silicon and neocloud challengers founded by two Stanford professors are together worth about one per cent of what the incumbents plan to spend in one year.

AI for drug discovery & molecules

What is observably happening

Stanford has both the method side (Ron Dror on ML plus molecular simulation, Possu Huang on protein design) and a commercial exit path that runs through a16z: Genesis Therapeutics came out of Vijay Pande's Stanford lab, and Pande is now a general partner at a16z, which led Genesis's Series A.

Who is paying

Pharma milestone money more than venture equity. Genesis has a Genentech partnership from 2020 and an Eli Lilly deal from 2022 with $20M upfront and up to $670M in potential milestones, against $304M of equity raised in total.

Market size, as published

2026 estimates for AI in drug discovery range from $2.9B (Grand View Research) to $4.0B (Global Market Insights) to $5.0B (Fortune Business Insights) to $24.51B (Towards Healthcare). That is an 8.4x spread between the lowest and highest published 2026 figure.

The arithmetic

Genesis at $806.79M is 27.8% of the lowest published 2026 market estimate and 3.3% of the highest. My calculation. The 8.4x disagreement between research firms is the finding: the category boundary (software tools only, versus the value of the resulting drug pipelines) is not agreed, so any single market-size number for this cluster is a choice of definition.

AI for clinical medicine

What is observably happening

This is the largest cluster by headcount and the smallest by disclosed market size. The School of Medicine alone accounts for 5,143 of Stanford's 7,394 faculty, and 917 Medicine faculty hold a Bio-X affiliation. James Zou's route is instructive: an FDA-cleared cardiac model (EchoNet) and a Nature paper first, a company second.

Who is paying

NIH and federal grants for the research; venture for the spinouts; and the health systems themselves as eventual buyers. Stanford's total sponsored research was $2.3B in the fiscal year ended 31 August 2025, with over 70% federally sponsored.

Market size, as published

AI in medical imaging in 2026 estimated at $2.20B (Coherent Market Insights) and $2.4B (a second firm), with CAGRs of 26.7%-34.8% to 2033.

The arithmetic

Human Intelligence targeting roughly $1B while raising about $100M equals 42%-45% of the entire 2026 imaging-AI market size, at a company with no disclosed revenue. My calculation. The gap between 4,929 faculty whose stated interests touch health and a $2.2B software market is the structural fact of this cluster: the research supply is enormous and the software market it feeds is small.

Neurotechnology & brain interfaces

What is observably happening

Stanford's position here is the clinical evidence base rather than an equity stake. The Neural Prosthetics Translational Lab, founded in 2009 by Krishna Shenoy (died 2023) and Jaimie Henderson, set the intracortical BCI communication records; Henderson and Frank Willett are now named research partners of Paradromics, not founders of it.

Who is paying

A mix that is unusual in this map: Paradromics holds $18M in NIH and DARPA grants alongside $105M+ of venture. Science Corp's Series C investors include IQT, the CIA-linked venture arm, alongside Lightspeed and Khosla.

Market size, as published

Brain-computer interface market in 2026 estimated at $2.75B (Coherent Market Insights) and $3.75B (Precedence Research), growing at 14.6%-16.7%.

The arithmetic

Total disclosed private capital in the two named companies is roughly $613M ($490M plus $123M), against a $2.75B-$3.75B stated 2026 market. My calculation. Note the mismatch with the clinical reality: the field's headline results are single-digit patient counts, so the 'market' figure is not being generated by implanted-device revenue.

Energy storage, fusion & materials

What is observably happening

One Stanford professor, Yi Cui, has founded five companies (Amprius, EnerVenue, EEnotech, 4C Air, LifeLabs Design) and is faculty director of the Sustainability Accelerator, which has awarded nearly $5M across teams in 22 departments. Separately, Mike Dunne left the directorship of SLAC's Linac Coherent Light Source to co-found a fusion company.

Who is paying

For storage, public markets and defence/aviation customers: Amprius trades on the NYSE and sells into aviation and defense. For fusion, milestone-based venture: Bessemer led Inertia's $450M Series A with GV participating. Precourt Institute for Energy has 116 faculty and only 4 of them also hold an HAI affiliation.

Market size, as published

Grid-scale battery storage in 2026 estimated at $8.32B, $11.8B, $16.9B and $19.09B by four different firms, a 2.3x spread. Physical deployment is less ambiguous: 353.4 GWh of storage expected to be added in 2026, and BESS shipments rose 75.5% to 421.2 GWh in 2025 with roughly 600 GWh projected for 2026.

The arithmetic

Amprius revenue went from roughly $24M implied in 2024 to $73M in 2025 to company guidance above $125M for 2026, i.e. roughly 3x then roughly 1.7x. My calculation from the reported figures. Against 600 GWh of projected 2026 BESS shipments, the deployment series is growing faster than the dollar market-size estimates suggest, because cell prices are falling.

Space & defence autonomy

What is observably happening

Stanford's aero/astro faculty work on the technical layer (Simone D'Amico on rendezvous, Manan Arya and Maria Sakovsky on deployable structures, Grace Gao on navigation) while the capital has gone to alumni-founded companies. Reported student preference has shifted: Palantir, Anduril and defense-selling startups are described as the sought-after destinations.

Who is paying

Quantum & photonics

What is observably happening

Stanford has the device physics (Jelena Vuckovic's Nanoscale and Quantum Photonics Lab, Amir Safavi-Naeini's LINQS Lab) and a joint Stanford-SLAC initiative in Q-FARM, but no large Stanford-founded quantum company surfaced in this pass. Of the 21 faculty whose stated interests mention quantum methods, the commercialisation path is not visible in the funding record.

Who is paying

Federal science funding and national quantum centres (Stanford Applied Physics faculty are founding members of the Q-NEXT national centre), plus Q-FARM's own seed-grant programme. Not venture, at least not yet.

Market size, as published

2026 quantum computing market estimated at $1.82B, $1.9B and $5.09B by three firms. QED-C's State of the Global Quantum Industry 2026 report projects the market doubling by 2028 to reach $3B in revenue.

The arithmetic

Two of the three 2026 estimates ($1.82B, $1.9B) are consistent with QED-C's $3B-by-2028 projection; the third ($5.09B for 2026) already exceeds the 2028 projection. My observation: the 2.8x spread and the internal contradiction mean the quantum market figures cannot all be measuring the same thing, and the honest read is QED-C's, which is revenue rather than 'market'.

Seed funds and angels, and in several cases Y Combinator. StartX itself is unusual: no fee, no equity, funded through the Stanford-StartX Fund structure.

How this was built

So you can check it, rebuild it, or disagree with a specific step.

The census

Stanford Profiles exposes a browse endpoint per organisational unit that accepts an affiliation filter. Pulling ?affiliations=capFaculty across 6 schools, SLAC, the Vice Provost and Dean of Research office and 13 institutes returned 12,827 rows, which dedupe by profile slug to 7,394 unique faculty. Parse rate against Stanford's own stated result counts was 99.8%. Each record carries name, full appointment title, and the free-text research-interest field where the person filled it in (4,369 of 7,394).

The nodes

The 86 detailed nodes were assembled from four pages fetched directly this session: the Stanford Robotics Center faculty roster (42 faculty), the SAIL faculty list (32), the AIMI centre, and the institutes. Lab names and lab URLs appear only where that page named them. Every person link is Stanford's own profile URL, taken from the census, so it resolves.

Known gaps, stated

Faculty missing from the census. The browse filter returns people carrying a faculty affiliation in the units queried. Dan Jurafsky, an active CS and Linguistics professor, does not appear in the harvest at all. So the census is a floor even at the person level.

One title per person. Where someone holds several appointments, the harvested title is whichever the listing showed. Christopher Manning's record reads as Linguistics emeritus faculty; he is also an active CS professor and SAIL director. Titles are Stanford's text, not corrected by me.

Keyword layer is weak evidence. The method and domain tags are regex over the research-interest field. Precision on the cross-domain cells ran about two in three in my sample. The institute co-affiliation matrix has no such problem and should be preferred.

Market sizes are third-party. Where research firms disagree the spread is shown, up to 8.4x in AI drug discovery. No single number here is mine except the explicitly labelled arithmetic, which is addition and division over cited figures.

Not covered in this pass. Grant-level dollar amounts per lab, affiliate-programme fee schedules beyond HAI's stated $550K research-token wallet, and the full StartX and alumni company set. Those were deferred to a second pass.
