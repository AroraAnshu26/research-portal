Five things in here that change the picture

1. Sunday Robotics has one co-founder from each lab. Tony Z. Zhao (IRIS, dropped out of his PhD in 2024, author of ALOHA and ACT) is CEO. Cheng Chi (REALab, Columbia PhD under Song, author of Diffusion Policy and the Universal Manipulation Interface) is CTO. $165M Series B led by Coatue at a $1.15B valuation, reported 12 March 2026 — roughly fifteen months from founding. Two more REALab people, Hojung Choi and Alper Canberk, are there too. If you want the single clearest proof that these two labs produce companies, it is one company with two founders.
2. Someone is already building your thesis, and he came out of IRIS. Govind Chada, an IRIS undergraduate alumnus, is Founder and CTO of Enact in YC's Summer 2026 batch. Their own description: “the post-training layer for robotics” — deploy policies on physical robots, find failure states, recreate them, collect recovery demonstrations, retrain. They quote 99 of 100 rollouts against a 90 of 100 baseline. This is simultaneously the strongest validation that the problem is real and the clearest evidence that you are not early.
3. The faculty who match your thesis are not in CS. Your problem is statistical validation of a stochastic policy. The people who own that at Stanford are Mykel Kochenderfer in Aero/Astro, who wrote Algorithms for Validation (MIT Press, 2026) and whose earlier work became the FAA's ACAS X certification basis; Mac Schwager, also Aero/Astro, a co-author of the tighter-than-Clopper-Pearson bounds your own arithmetic rests on; Somil Bansal, Aero/Astro, on safety filters; and Emma Brunskill in CS, whose field is literally off-policy evaluation without a simulator. Neither Finn nor Song is on that list.
4. Almost none of the robotics faculty have founded anything — and the most commercially experienced person at the Robotics Center is not faculty. Khatib, Cutkosky, Okamura, Kochenderfer, Schwager, Sadigh, Bohg, C. Karen Liu, Jiajun Wu and Pavone have founded a combined total of zero companies. What they hold is lineage: Khatib trained the CEO of Dexterity ($1.65B). The person who has actually founded, raised, handed off a CEO seat and industrialised spinouts is Steve Cousins, the Robotics Center's Executive Director — ex-CEO of Willow Garage, which gave ROS away for free and spun out eight companies. He has lived both sides of your commons problem.
5. A brand-new lab with your exact background arrives in about six weeks. Thomas Berrueta starts as Assistant Professor of Mechanical Engineering on 1 November 2026 (his own site and the ME department agree; the Robotics Center roster says January 2027 and is wrong). His stated foundations are reinforcement learning, optimal control and information theory — not an approximation of your background, an exact match. His PAL Lab page is already live and he has no students.

The two labs are not the same kind of place

This is the comparison that actually bears on your decision, and it did not come from either lab's marketing — it came from counting what the members publish and who pays them.

IRIS — Chelsea Finn — 32 members

A third of the PhD students no longer do robotics. Yoonho Lee, Anikait Singh and Jubayer Ibn Hamid work entirely on language-model post-training with no robot at all. Moo Jin Kim, who first-authored OpenVLA, has moved to LLM post-training for Marin. That is four of twelve PhD students off the physical-AI axis, which is why several IRIS scores here are low on startup relevance despite strong papers — the buyers are real but they are not robotics buyers.
The Physical Intelligence conflict is structural, not incidental. Finn co-founded PI. Lucy Shi has been a core PI member since March 2024, Marcel Torne was there nine months, Alexander Swerdlow interned there, and Ajay Sridhar's MemER is built on pi-0.5. Your thesis says an independent evaluator is adversarial to policy vendors, and the sharpest honest findings in your direction are adverse to PI specifically. This is the one topic where your incentives and the lab's diverge.
The PI entanglement runs deeper than the roster suggests. Checking author lists rather than affiliations: MEM (Marcel Torne) carries nine Physical Intelligence-affiliated co-authors, Hi Robot (Lucy Shi) carries seven, and OpenVLA four. So the memory and long-horizon thrust — the one I call the most attractive thin thrust below — is itself partly a PI collaboration. The exception matters: Freeform Preference Learning, the single most thesis-adjacent paper in IRIS, is Torne plus two MS students plus Finn, with no PI people at all. If you want the thin thrust without the conflict, it is that paper, not MEM.
Two thrusts are thin and attractive. Memory and long-horizon control has only Marcel Torne, Ajay Sridhar and one undergraduate, and the three papers genuinely disagree about the answer. Human preference learning is Torne plus two MS students. Evaluation and verification looks healthy on the publications page but IRIS members are middle authors on most of it, and the only person who owns any of it — Yuejiang Liu — leaves for a faculty post at NUS in 2027.

REALab — Shuran Song — 19 members

One hundred per cent robotics. No LLM drift anywhere. Every one of the 91 papers on the lab page is about physical manipulation. The dominant themes are exactly the ones your market map named as blockers: compliance and contact-rich control (Adaptive Compliance Policy, Compliant Residual DAgger, UMI-FT), cheap data collection (the whole UMI lineage), and hardware-software co-design.
No policy-vendor conflict. The industry channels here are NVIDIA (Yihuai Gao, on Cosmos Policy and Cosmos 3), Amazon FAR (Xiaomeng Xu), Boston Dynamics (Chuer Pan, on the electric Atlas), Meta (Zeyi Liu), 1X (Juntao Ren, on world models) and Anthropic (two alumni). Not Physical Intelligence. An independent-evaluation thesis is not adversarial to anyone's interests here.
Its artifacts actually escaped. Diffusion Policy and the Universal Manipulation Interface both came out of this lab and became field defaults, and then became Sunday Robotics. That is the strongest available evidence that work done here reaches products rather than only citations.
Two people you would not expect. Jisang Park is an MSCS student who co-founded three startups and came through McKinsey and BCG before this, explicitly to commercialise robotics. Han Zhang is a first-year PhD who already co-founded Dexta Robotics. Both are living answers to questions you are currently asking in the abstract.

What the alumni distribution actually says

Founding is the exception, not the path. Of everyone traced, 12 are founders. The dominant IRIS PhD outcome is frontier-lab research staff — OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Thinking Machines, Meta — and three of those four are on post-training specifically, because the DPO lineage pushed the whole cohort there. The dominant REALab outcome is robotics research at NVIDIA, Tesla, Meta and Anthropic. If your objective is to found a company, neither lab's modal outcome gets you there; the founders mostly LEFT early. Tony Zhao dropped out in his third year. Khazatsky's LinkedIn shows Stanford ending in 2024. Govind Chada and Sergio Charles founded as undergraduate alumni.
And the range of outcomes is enormous. Sunday Robotics reached $1.15B in about fifteen months. Emancro, founded by Frederik Ebert in 2022 on the same kind of research, is still at a $3.5M seed with two to three employees three years later. Both are real founders from the same lineage. Treat the Sunday number as the tail, not the expectation.

Huaxiu Yao was reported as a co-founder of Ricursive Intelligence — it is Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini, and he has no company at all. Moo Jin Kim was reported as co-founder and CTO of CollectedAI — zero corroboration; the claim was hallucinated by a search summary. Kenneth Salisbury did NOT found Intuitive Surgical, despite the folklore — he was its scientific advisor from 1997 to 2003.
