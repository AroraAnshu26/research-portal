    How this works

    The columns are the mandate. Money flows, macro cycles, geopolitics × tech, what is genuinely new, and theses. Every piece of research lands in one of them, so a gap in the worldview shows up as a short column rather than as a vague feeling.

    Reports are files, not records. Anything in reports/ shows up on the Reports tab by itself. Drop a PDF, a markdown file or a self-contained HTML dashboard in there and it is in the portal. Nothing to register.

    The scratchpad is one file per day. logs/anshu/2026-09-10.md and so on. Plain markdown, saved automatically. Your co-founder gets logs/veer/ — separate directory, same portal, no collision.

    Agents are charters before they are code. Six standing briefs are written out on the Agents tab, each naming its sources, its cadence, what its output must contain, and what it must never do. Two of them need paid or authorised access before they can run; one — the YC batch delta — only needs wiring, because the scraper and classifier already exist. Until then, "Copy charter" puts the whole brief on your clipboard to paste into a session.

    The shared copy. aroraanshu26.github.io/research-portal is public and read-only: the board, the reports and the day logs all read normally, and notes a visitor types stay in their own browser. Double-click Publish.cmd to push an update. To see exactly what a visitor sees, open ?static=1 on the local copy.

    When the co-founder starts writing. The author selector, the per-author log directories and the owner field on every card are already in place. Two people writing at once means running the same server.mjs somewhere you both reach, instead of the read-only page — no restructuring, no data migration.

    If the server is not running, the portal still opens and everything reads normally; the scratchpad falls back to browser storage and the status chip at the top turns amber. Every save also writes to browser storage even when the server is up, so the two never diverge in a way that loses text.

    The five house rules the research follows
    1. Lay out the facts and the observations. No cognition-by-proxy: no ambition scores, no recommended verdicts, no selling.
    2. Quantities over adjectives — counts, shares, dates, dollar figures, who pays. Estimates labelled as estimates, with the basis.
    3. Observations stated as observations. "X went from 12% to 31% of the batch," not "you should look at X."
    4. Recommendations only when explicitly asked for.
    5. Pain is only real when learned from someone already spending money badly on it. Never from a friend, never from a warm intro.
